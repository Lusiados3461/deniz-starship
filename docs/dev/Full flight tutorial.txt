1. CHOOSE THE RIGHT CRAFT
Start with Lusiados Starship 01 - Empty - Tower Launch to learn the whole stack. Use Lusiados Starship 02 - Hubble Telescope - Clamshell - Tower Launch once you understand deployment.
Tower Launch has the twin towers and moving support arms. Historical Full Stack and Ship Only versions are retired; they remain recoverable from Backups. All missions use Tower Launch. A test driver may explicitly generate a ZZ TEMP ship-only variant for hops or inserted-orbit regression, then archive it at the endpoint.
Use the dedicated Lusiados Starship Missions Test save for practice. Assign two crew to the carrier cabin in the VAB Crew tab. Assign extra crew to payload seats only if that mission needs them; a carrier crew member is not automatically assigned to a separate payload cabin.

2. KNOW THE CONTROLS
Number row:
1 = toggle cargo open/closed.
2 = toggle cargo lock.
3 or G = toggle carrier landing legs.
4 = toggle carrier leg lock.
5 = toggle carrier entry/landing flap preset.
6 = carrier landing engines: ship sea-level engines, booster centre cluster.
7 = carrier flight engines: all ship engines, full booster cluster.
8 = toggle deployment arm extension/retraction.
9 = release cargo only while coasting, actual main thrust zero/RCS off, bay fully open and arm fully extended/locked. Main-thrust release is refused without changing throttle.
0 = deploy active vessel's equipment. Aboard Starship this does not unfold the attached cargo's equipment.

After switching to a released payload:
7 = activate its engine, if fitted.
6 = shut its engine down.
5 = arm/deploy its parachutes, if fitted.
0 = deploy its equipment.
G = payload legs, where fitted.
B = rover brakes.
These payload engine/parachute commands are blocked while cargo is still attached.

Flight keys on this Mac:
Space = next stage in staging mode.
T = SAS; R = RCS; U = lights.
W/S = pitch; A/D = yaw; Q/E = roll.
Shift = increase throttle; left Control = decrease throttle.
Z = full throttle; X = zero throttle.
Return = fine controls, with blue control indicators. This is the current saved binding.
H/N = forward/back translation; I/K = down/up; J/L = left/right.
O/P, or [ and ], switch nearby vessels.
F5 = quicksave when allowed; hold F9 = quickload. Fn may be needed for Mac function keys.

The number keys, 3/G, 4, 5 and 8 are toggles: read the status message rather than pressing repeatedly. Cargo starts locked; legs start unlocked. Wait for doors/legs to stop before locking. Engine keys 6/7 activate engines: set X before changing modes if you want them to remain at zero thrust.

3. PREPARE FUEL AND LANDING MASS
For ordinary missions, use the normal fuel load and plan reserves for separation, boostback, deorbit and landing. There is no proven reserve percentage for every payload.
For your infinite-fuel practice, reduce both LqdMethane and Oxidizer by the same percentage in the VAB. Empty ship hops used approximately 20 percent; the separate booster hop used 5 percent. These are verified hop loads, not validated full-stack orbital settings for every payload. Heavier cargo needs its own thrust-to-weight check.
Infinite fuel preserves tank contents and their mass. A full booster can remain too heavy for its centre landing engines even though it cannot run out of fuel.
TWR means thrust divided by weight. At maximum usable thrust, a local landing TWR below 1 cannot arrest vertical descent. Aim above 1.2, preferably nearer 1.5–2 for learning. Check at the relevant atmospheric pressure, with the intended engines selected. If mode 6 is insufficient, keep more engines active with 7, use a lighter starting load, or burn down with infinite fuel disabled before attempting the return.

4. PREPARE THE PAD AND DISPLAYS
Cargo closed and locked. Arm retracted. Legs retracted. Cargo panels folded. Throttle zero. SAS attitude hold on. RCS initially off unless needed.
In Kerbal Engineer, display apoapsis, periapsis, time to apoapsis, altitude above terrain, vertical speed, horizontal speed, TWR and landing/suicide-burn readouts where available. Verify selected engines and atmospheric settings when interpreting a stage TWR estimate. Sea-level altitude is not clearance above the ground.
Arm FMRS on the pad if you want both Starship and Super Heavy recovered. It records controllable separated stages so you can finish the main flight, then replay booster recovery from separation. This avoids trying to pilot both falling vehicles at once.
Disable Booster Guidance, MechJeb Landing Guidance, Translatron and competing attitude controllers before ascent.

5. LAUNCH AND REACH ORBIT
Suggested first orbit: 150 km, inclination 0 degrees from KSC. This gives a roomy equatorial orbit for deployment. Due-east heading is 90 degrees.
If using MechJeb Ascent Guidance, use Classic Ascent Profile as a starting point:
- Orbit altitude 150 km; inclination 0 degrees.
- Auto-stage OFF; hot staging OFF; Auto-warp OFF; Auto-deploy solar panels OFF.
- Skip circularization OFF.
- Edit Ascent Path: turn start around 1 km, turn end around 45 km, turn shape 40 percent, final angle 0 degrees.
- Start with an AoA limit around 5 degrees and an acceleration limit around 20 m/s². These are suggested settings for tuning, not tested fleet presets. If it tumbles, reduce the turn demand and keep its nose closer to prograde.

Initially launch manually. Apply enough throttle to lift with a gentle positive acceleration; a liftoff TWR around 1.3–1.6 is a useful aim. Light infinite-fuel loads may need far less than full throttle. Press Space ONCE.
Tower Launch starts Super Heavy, opens the support arms, then releases the mount after the arms clear. Wait several seconds. Do not press Space again to make it release faster. If the mount reports that the arms did not clear, stop thrust and inspect the tower rather than forcing separation.
After clearing the tower, engage Ascent Guidance if using it. It controls steering AND throttle during ascent. Leave staging manual.

For manual ascent, rise vertically clear of the stand, then gradually pitch toward heading 90. Make small turns and stay close to surface prograde in dense air. Continue building horizontal speed as altitude increases. Use apoapsis, not current altitude, to decide when to stop the climb. Reduce/cut thrust when apoapsis approaches 150 km, coast toward it, then burn orbital prograde around apoapsis until periapsis is safely above 70 km. Aim for roughly 150 x 150 km. High altitude alone does not make an orbit.

6. SEPARATE THE BOOSTER
Separate on a rising suborbital trajectory after it has provided enough lift for Starship to finish. Above the 70 km atmosphere is a convenient infinite-fuel training handoff; actual separation depends on fuel, velocity, payload and remaining ship capability. Do not use a fixed real-world elapsed time.
If MechJeb controls throttle, disengage it briefly first. Press X, then Space ONCE for the second staging event. The booster separates and its engines shut down; Starship's engines activate. Let the vessels separate, then resume throttle/Ascent Guidance on Starship.
Check FMRS has recorded the booster. Complete Starship's orbit first. Ensure both apoapsis and periapsis remain above the atmosphere. Save once stable and guidance is disengaged.

7. RECOVER SUPER HEAVY WITH FMRS
Use its FMRS vessel entry to jump back to separation. Confirm you control Deniz Super Heavy. O/P or brackets are useful for nearby switching but do not preserve both flights through time the way FMRS does. After landing the booster, use FMRS to return to the main flight and merge the recovery result.

Manual boostback:
- Keep throttle zero and enable SAS/RCS. Use full flight engines with 7 if needed.
- Select the actual landing area in Trajectories. For learning, use clear flat ground near KSC rather than the occupied launch mount.
- Turn to oppose your downrange motion, then burn while watching the predicted impact point. A controlled retrograde boostback initially cancels eastward speed; more burn can send you west toward KSC. Add crossrange correction only if the prediction needs it.
- Stop when the predicted path reaches the desired area. Coast and point opposite surface velocity for atmospheric entry. Use small corrections and keep the heat/attitude under control.
- Choose 6 for landing only when the centre cluster has sufficient TWR. Extend and lock legs with 3, wait, then 4. Use the manual landing method in step 11.

Optional Booster Guidance:
Its target and Trajectories' target are separate settings. Pick the location in Booster Guidance or target the drone ship and verify the copied coordinates. Target altitude must represent the terrain/deck elevation above sea level, not a desired hover height.
Disable competing MechJeb autopilots and Smart A.S.S.; keep SAS available. Set the desired landing engines at zero throttle with 6, check TWR, and use Landing Burn's Set control to record them. Restore 7 for a stronger boostback if necessary. Enable Guidance while still on the outbound suborbital return, preferably above 70 km. Verify the selected phase is Boostback. If it skips boostback, do not assume the return has been done; investigate the phase before descent.
It controls both throttle and steering. Check RCS is ON after enabling because this version may toggle its state. Let phases advance normally. Deploy and lock legs yourself. Disable Guidance before taking over manually. Its recovery on this custom Super Heavy is unvalidated, and it is not a Starship belly-flop controller.

Recorded9October C1 full-return and pad-hop contact/support/reload/release phases passed with disclosed saved-source/FMRS replay and initial pad-settling limits. The module closes and joins the arms; the pilot still flies boostback, hover and powered clearance. Read C1 tower catch.txt before enabling brackets or calibrating the tower. 8. DELIVER ORBITAL CARGO
Choose a stable orbit with periapsis above the atmosphere. Disable ascent/maneuver autopilots. Set X and stop rotation; use SAS attitude hold. Face the bay toward empty space, clear of nearby vessels. Avoid time warp while doors and robotics move.
Cut actual main thrust to zero and turn RCS off. After a powered arm check, switch moving prograde/maneuver tracking OFF and use stock SAS Stability Assist to hold a fixed attitude; let rotation and flex settle before release, then retain that hold until stowed. Verified in flight9October: Heavy Tug powered refusal then coast release passed with3continuous normal seconds at angular speed<0.0002rad/s and zero thrust before release, contact0/all39originalparts. The prior continuously prograde-tracked retry recorded two gentle engine-to-clamshell contact times and remains failed for clearance. Evidence: work/starship-pack/evidence/2026-10-09-C1-tower-catch/payload-tug-powered-inertial-coast-03. This is the tested operational bound, not proof of every attitude or full-throttle arm load. Press2 to unlock,1 to open fully,8 to extend fully and lock,9 to release. Maintain coast until stowed; do not accelerate into the payload.
Clamshell carriers retract, wait until the released payload clears the swept nose envelope, close and lock. Unknown/near payload keeps the nose open; manual close is also guarded. Stay nearby and verify completion before leaving physics range. After reload, right-click cargo and choose Prepare for return: stow and close to restart; saved guard references persist, coroutine continuation is unaccepted.
Side-door carriers need 8 to retract, then 1 to close, then 2 to lock, with a wait after each moving step.
Use O/P or brackets to control the released payload. Keep its engine off initially. Use gentle RCS translation to move clear; deploy large equipment with 0 after gaining clearance. Start an engine with 7 only once its exhaust and vehicle are clear. Key 6 stops the payload engine. F5 after a successful clean deployment.
If it is meant to stay in orbit, verify the payload's own apoapsis/periapsis. Separation alone does not guarantee an independent stable orbit.

9. USE EACH MISSION DESIGN
Empty / Empty Large Bay: attach your own cargo to the release collar. Fold appendages, preserve a control point/power, keep the cabin accessible, and test a complete deployment on the ground before launch. A payload shape change invalidates the existing clearance test for that payload.
Satellite: deploy in the required orbit; activate panels/antenna. Use its small engine for orbit adjustments.
Hubble Telescope: deploy from the clamshell, translate clear, press 0, and use the SpaceDust telescope's right-click observation controls. The instrument optical hatch is separate from Starship's clamshell.
Station Builder / Station Hub: deploy in orbit; use docking ports and RCS to join another station module. They are orbital modules, not surface landers.
Refuelling / Orbital Fuel Depot: rendezvous and dock; transfer matching methane and oxidizer resources. Ordinary LiquidFuel does not fuel the Starship methalox engines.
Mun Rover / Expedition Rover: deliver to the Mun or another suitable low-gravity destination. The small Mun rover has a descent engine. The Expedition Rover is a wheeled vehicle and requires a delivery plan; do not assume it has the same self-landing hardware.
Duna Outpost / Surface Base: deliver to a suitable surface with the payload's engine, legs and any fitted parachutes. Check local TWR. The large base is intended for low-gravity delivery; its engine-only Kerbin landing is not established.
Crew Rescue / Crew Shuttle: assign/transfer crew to the correct separate seats; rendezvous with the rescue target or use the shuttle as a lander. Check engines/fuel for that payload's destination and return.
Asteroid Tug / Heavy Asteroid Tug: rendezvous, match relative velocity, arm its grappling claw in the right-click menu, approach slowly and capture. A captured asteroid's mass changes handling and TWR enormously.

10. DOCK, REFUEL OR TRAVEL TO THE MUN/DUNA
For rendezvous, target the vessel, match orbital planes if needed, plan the encounter, then match target-relative velocity at closest approach. MechJeb Maneuver Planner supplies these node types. Target retrograde is useful for matching another spacecraft's velocity; it does not guide a surface landing.
Near docking, right-click the Starship tank and select RCS: docking precision. Enable R and Return fine controls. Set the target docking port as target and your own port as the control point. Use short H/N/I/J/K/L taps. Aim below about 0.2 m/s near contact. Confirm docking before opening resource transfer menus. Use the saved left-Alt modifier as applicable to resource selection, or the resource panel.
Mun: start from the stable Kerbin orbit, target the Mun and use a suitable moon-transfer node (Bi-Impulsive/Hohmann transfer, depending on installed menu). Check it actually predicts a Mun encounter before execution. Correct the encounter to pass safely above terrain, then capture with a retrograde burn at periapsis. Circularize before starting a separate surface descent. The Mun has no atmospheric braking or useful parachutes: land with engines using local TWR and terrain altitude. Return planning can use Return from a moon.
Duna: plan a transfer at a suitable window with Advanced transfer to another planet; confirm an encounter, perform a course correction, then use a controlled capture into a safe orbit. Engine capture is a straightforward infinite-fuel practice option. For surface descent, the atmosphere is thin: parachutes may help, but retain engine capability and do not rely on a fixed Kerbin deployment altitude. Plan ascent and refuelling/reserves if the carrier is returning.
For surface cargo release, first land Starship upright on level ground with a clear outward bay path. Transfer payload crew before release. Extend the arm, release, immediately switch to the payload and control its engine if fitted; otherwise provide a safe lowering/delivery method. Do not drop the wheeled Expedition Rover from the full bay height onto Kerbin and expect a gentle landing.
Full docking and Mun/Duna delivery have not been flight-tested for this fleet.

11. RETURN AND LAND STARSHIP MANUALLY
For controlled hop tests, first learn with a temporary ZZ TEMP ship-only blueprint and an empty ship; archive the blueprint after the task. An upright powered return teaches braking; a full belly-flop/flip is a further unvalidated flight exercise.
Before deorbit, retract the arm, close and lock cargo, retract vulnerable external equipment through its part menu, and check engine mode 6 provides adequate landing TWR. Set your landing location in Trajectories. Its prediction depends on the attitude/descent profile and current vessel configuration; parachutes and future staging are not predicted.
From orbit, point orbital retrograde and make a small deorbit burn. A periapsis around 30–40 km can be a training starting point from low Kerbin orbit, but tune the burn using the atmospheric prediction rather than assuming this guarantees KSC. Perform the burn early enough that the path intersects the desired longitude. Stop and watch the prediction; later small burns can shorten a path that overshoots. Make crossrange corrections while there is time and altitude.
Use a shallow, controlled entry and watch heating. For upright entry, configure a retrograde descent prediction and hold surface retrograde once entering the atmosphere. Orbital retrograde and surface retrograde are different in a rotating atmosphere. Smart A.S.S. SURF -> SVEL- can hold the latter while you operate throttle. It points opposite surface motion; it does not itself steer to the landing target. Avoid competing attitude controllers.
For belly-first practice, press 5 and verify Entry flap preset, manually present the heat-shielded side to airflow and configure Trajectories for that actual orientation. The key changes flap angles; it does not pitch the ship horizontal or execute a flip. Begin practicing the upright transition several kilometres high with a generous stopping margin. Exact flip timing depends on descent speed, mass, thrust and rotation time and is not established for this craft.
For the powered final approach, disable ascent/landing/Booster Guidance throttle control. At zero throttle select 6, press 5 if needed for Landing flap preset, extend legs with 3 and lock with 4 after extension. Arrest sideways motion while high enough, then finish upright; once upright, SAS attitude hold avoids chasing a wandering retrograde marker at very low speed.
Use altitude ABOVE TERRAIN, vertical speed and usable TWR to start braking early. KER's landing estimate is an aid, not a guaranteed trigger. Do not use one fixed burn altitude for every payload. A simplified upright stopping distance is v²/[2*g*(TWR-1)], using local full-thrust TWR and downward speed, ignoring changing drag/mass and turn time. At 150 m/s on Kerbin, TWR 2 needs roughly 1.15 km to stop even before margin; TWR 1.5 needs roughly 2.3 km. Start well above this and account for time spent turning.
Burn firmly to reduce a fast descent, then lower throttle before vertical speed reaches zero. The approximate upright hover setting is 1/TWR: TWR 2 corresponds to about 50 percent throttle. This is only a starting estimate; use the actual speed trend. Aim roughly 5–10 m/s down with comfortable ground clearance, 1–3 m/s near the final tens of metres, then below about 1 m/s for contact. Keep sideways speed very low. Cut thrust with X only when the legs have contacted and the vehicle has settled. Leave legs locked; reduce unnecessary high-power RCS corrections after touchdown.
Choose level ground near KSC for the first return. Accuracy improves with repeated entries using the same fuel mass, cargo and descent attitude. Infinite fuel allows early braking, but provides no protection from heating, impact or loss of control.

12. QUICK PROBLEMS
Door does not open: cargo locked; use 2 once and verify Unlocked before 1.
Door cannot close: arm still extended; retract and wait. Never force it through cargo.
9 does nothing: bay not fully open or arm not fully extended; read the status.
Legs do not move: unlock with 4, then use 3/G. Lock only after motion stops.
Engine mode causes a sudden leap: throttle was already raised; X before 6/7.
Maximum throttle cannot stop falling: insufficient local TWR, wrong engines, severe tilt, or no propellant. More throttle cannot fix TWR below 1.
Throttle changes without input: ascent, landing, Booster Guidance or another throttle controller is engaged. Disable it before manual landing.
Cargo engine does not start: still attached, wrong active vessel, or that payload has no engine.
Target is selected but landing misses: attitude hold is not trajectory correction; check actual impact prediction and the selected guidance phase/coordinates.
Fine controls do not react to Caps Lock: current Mac binding is Return.
Ship rebounds from landing: braking stayed too high or late; lower throttle toward a controlled slow descent before contact.
